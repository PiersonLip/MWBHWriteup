# The kicks decide who stays together

*04 · Natal kicks & disruption*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
When a star in a binary dies in the form of a [[supernova]], two things mess with the orbit. The sudden extreme mass loss shifts the center of mass (referred to as the [[Blaauw effect|Blaauw effect or Blaauw kick]]) and any sort of asymmetries in the supernova can also give the remnant an extra [[natal kick]][^2]. If the combined effect of these is strong enough, the stars are ejected and the system becomes [[disrupted]]. This disruption from strong kicks is a big reason so many BHs are single, and why low-mass BHs are especially likely to be isolated[^1]. 
:::

[^1]: Lower remnant mass means a larger kick velocity for the same kick strength.

[^2]: When people typically think of supernovae, we naively generally think of a radially symmetric explosion coming from the star. However in reality these explosions are typically stronger in one direction, causing a kick from the "recoil" in the opposite direction.
