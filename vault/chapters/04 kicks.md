# The kick decides who stays together

*04 · Natal kicks & disruption*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
When a star in a binary dies in the form of a [[supernova]], two things mess with the orbit. The sudden extreme mass loss shifts the center of mass (the [[Blaauw effect]]) and any sort of asymmetries can also give the remnant an extra [[natal kick]][^2]. If the combined effect of these is strong enough, the system becomes [[disrupted]]. This disruption from strong kicks is a big reason so many BHs are single, and why low-mass BHs are especially likely to be isolated[^1]. 
:::

[^1]: a lower mass means a harder kick

[^2]: When we think of supernovae, we typically think of a radially symmetric explosion coming from the star. However, these explosions are typically stronger in one a direction, causing a kick from the "recoil" in the opposite direction.
