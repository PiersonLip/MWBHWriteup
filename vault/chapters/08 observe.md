# What Gaia and Roman can actually test

*08 · Observational links*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
**Gaia BH+star systems.** Quiet black holes with luminous companions are rare in the full census, but they're gold for astrometry. POSYDON fills the middle ground between tight post-CE systems and wide non-interacting binaries with a solid [stable mass transfer]{tip: Mass transfer that stays under control instead of running away into a common envelope.} population. Rapid codes mostly miss that middle. Gaia BH1 and BH2 sit near it, which makes them look a little less like weirdos in mass-period space (matching every observed detail is still tough).

**Roman microlensing of BH-BH lenses.** After parking populations in a Galactic model and doing a simple Einstein-radius cut, about **~10%** of bulge-pointed detached BH-BH systems land in the sweet spot for binary-lens signatures (0.7 < θ<sub>sep</sub>/θ̃<sub>E</sub> < 1.8). If you can get component masses, the low-mass stable-MT pile-up and the ~13 M☉ wind feature become real observational tests.

These are intentionally rough links. Real survey forecasts need selection functions (gaiamock, PopSyCLE, and friends) on top of the kinematically evolved catalogs the paper already built.
:::

:::callout
Across core-collapse variants, the qualitative map holds: **low-mass BH-BH ≈ stable mass transfer**; **narrow high-mass feature ≈ wind-driven non-interacting progenitors**. Metallicity and winds decide where that feature sits, or whether it shows up at all.
:::
