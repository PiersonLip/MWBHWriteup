:::prose
Quiet black holes with luminous companions[^1] are pretty rare in the full census, but they're extremtly useful when it comes to measuring black holes. This is because they're both much easier to spot due their bright companion, but also by measuring the effect the black hole has on its companion, we can  

Microlensing is a form of [[gravitational lensing]] where the brightness of a star increases dramitcally due to an object (in this case a black hole) passing infront and bending light around itself. This process is currently our only way of measuring isolated black hole[^2].
:::

:::prose
**Roman microlensing of BH-BH lenses.** Roman's bulge survey is built for this kind of event — including lenses that are themselves binaries. After parking populations in a Galactic model and doing a simple Einstein-radius cut, about **~10%** of bulge-pointed detached BH-BH systems land in the sweet spot for binary-lens signatures (0.7 < θ<sub>sep</sub>/θ̃<sub>E</sub> < 1.8). If you can get component masses, the low-mass stable-MT pile-up and the ~13 M☉ wind feature become real observational tests.

These are intentionally rough links. Real survey forecasts need selection functions (gaiamock, PopSyCLE, and friends) on top of the kinematically evolved catalogs the paper already built.
:::

:::callout
Across core-collapse variants, the qualitative map holds: **low-mass BH-BH ≈ stable mass transfer**; **narrow high-mass feature ≈ wind-driven non-interacting progenitors**. Metallicity and winds decide where that feature sits, or whether it shows up at all.
:::

[^1]: I.e. a black hole with a companion that is pretty much anything **except** a black hole, neutron star, or white dwarf.

[^2]: Only one black hole has been detected with this process so far by Hubble.
