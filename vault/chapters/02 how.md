# Fancy binaries in a simplified Galaxy

*02 · Methodology*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
In order to model all of these binaries in our galaxy, we use POSYDON, which evolves single stars and binaries from birth ([[ZAMS]]) all the way to their death over the scale of billions of years. We use a selection of simulations of **10 million binaries** at varying [[metallicity|metallicities]][^1] and scale their results up to a Milky Way-like galaxy. 

With these systems there is still a large amount of physics we don't fully understand, so we try variations and see what sticks best. The things that we tweak are: 
:::

:::timeline
- **Initial binary properties**: Masses, periods, mass ratios, and [[eccentricity|eccentricities]] drawn for the birth population.
- **[[Core collapse]]**: How the remnant mass is assigned when the iron core collapses.
- **[[natal kick|Natal Kicks]]**: Maxwellian scale, fallback, inverse-mass, or zero-kick prescriptions.
- **[[common envelope|Common envelope]] efficiency**: How much orbital energy unbinds the shared envelope (α<sub>CE</sub>).
- **[[Metallicity]]**: The Z grid that sets wind strength and which evolutionary paths are available.
:::

[^1]: Specifically 1Z⊙, 0.45Z⊙, 0.2Z⊙, 0.1Z⊙, and 0.01Z⊙
