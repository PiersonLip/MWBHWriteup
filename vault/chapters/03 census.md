# Most Galactic black holes are alone (but they weren't born that way)

*03 · Demographics*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
In our baseline solar-[[metallicity]][^1] run, we found about **400,000,000** stellar-mass black holes in the Galaxy. Roughly three-quarters of these are single today, while the rest still have their binary partners.
:::

:::stats
- ~4×10⁸ | Total Galactic BHs
- 78% | Are isolated
- 19% | In detached BH-BH binaries
:::

:::prose
For the isolated black holes, **stellar mergers** are the most common (42% of all BHs): two stars drift closer and closer together until they merge into one (often after a failed [[common envelope]]), the merger product later collapsing into a black hole. **Disrupted binaries** come next (27%): these are black holes where a [[natal kick]] and/or sudden mass loss rips the binary apart. This can happen at the first or second [[core collapse]]. Stars born single, BH+star mergers, and rare [[DCO]] mergers make up the rest.

The binary systems that survive are unique in their own right. [[Detached]] **BH-BH** systems alone are ~19% of all BHs. BH+NS, BH+WD, and BH+star systems together are only a few percent, yet this few percent are really the only ones we've observed so far.

:::
