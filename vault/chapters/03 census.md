# Most Galactic black holes are alone (but they usually weren't born that way)

*03 · Demographics*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
In the fiducial solar-metallicity run, you get about **4×10⁸** stellar-mass black holes in the Galaxy. Roughly three-quarters are single today. The rest still have a partner. That loneliness is usually earned: stellar mergers and kicked-apart binaries beat out stars that were born alone.
:::

:::stats
- ~4×10⁸ | Total Galactic BHs
- 78% | Isolated at 10 Gyr
- 19% | In detached BH-BH binaries
:::

:::prose
For the loners, **stellar mergers** win (42% of all BHs): two normal stars smash together (often after a failed common envelope), then the merger product later collapses. **Disrupted binaries** come next (27%): a [natal kick]{tip: An extra shove that a newborn neutron star or black hole can get from an asymmetric supernova.} and/or sudden mass loss rips the binary apart at the first or second [core collapse]{tip: The end of a massive star where the iron core collapses. This leaves a neutron star, a black hole, or sometimes nothing.}. Born-single stars, BH+star mergers, and rare [DCO]{tip: Double compact object: BH-BH, BH-NS, NS-NS, or similar pairs. If they are tight enough, gravitational waves can merge them.} mergers fill out the leftovers.

The binaries that survive look lopsided. [Detached]{tip: Not transferring mass right now. The two objects orbit quietly without an accretion disk.} **BH-BH** systems alone are ~19% of all BHs. BH+NS, BH+WD, and BH+star systems together are only a few percent. Funny enough, those rare ones are basically everything we've observed so far.

The mass spectra keep showing two fingerprints: a low-mass bump near ~2.5 to 5 M☉ (helped by [stable mass transfer]{tip: Mass transfer that in a state of equilibrium, leading to more long term transfer of mass instead of a merger.} before BH formation, plus the delayed explosion engine) and a skinny pile-up near **~13 M☉** from windy [Wolf-Rayet]{tip: Hot, helium-rich stars that have already lost their hydrogen envelopes. These stars have incredibly strong winds} evolution. Median masses land around ~8.2 M☉ (isolated) and ~12.5 M☉ (in binaries).
:::
