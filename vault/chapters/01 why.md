# We're about to find a whole hell of a lot more black holes

*01 · Motivation*

> Edit the blocks below. Interactive figure guts stay on the site; only captions / titles / hints sync.

:::prose
The Milky Way should have **hundreds of millions** of stellar-mass black holes. Yet almost none of them are visible. Until recently we were only aware of bright ones sitting in [[X-ray binaries]] (a few dozen with solid mass measurements), plus whatever [[LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA|LIGO/Virgo/KAGRA]] finds far away.

That's changing fast. [[Gaia]] already confirmed three sleepy black holes with bright companions (Gaia BH1, BH2, BH3). [[Roman]] should [[microlens]] hundreds more. Gaia DR4 is expected to add dozens of astrometric BH binaries. So the fun question is shifting from "find me one cool system" to "what does the whole population look like?"

You can't answer that by just counting corpses. Most massive stars grow up with a roommate. Stuff like [[mass transfer]], [[common envelope]]s, mergers, and supernova kicks decide whether a black hole ends up alone or paired, heavy or light, tight or wide. A lot of older population codes approximate that with fitting formulae. This paper uses [[POSYDON]] instead, which leans on detailed [[MESA]] binary grids.
:::

:::callout
**The basic idea:** take today's Galactic black holes, trace them back to how they formed, poke the uncertain physics to see what sticks, then ask (at a first-pass level) what Gaia and Roman might actually notice.
:::
