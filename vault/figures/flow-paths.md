# Flowchart path blurbs

These show in the **How do Galactic black holes form?** detail panel when you click a path.
Edit the paragraph under each heading, then run `python3 scripts/apply_vault.py`.

## merger · Stellar mergers

Two normal stars merge (typically after a failed [[common envelope]]), then the leftover collapses to a black hole. This is the most common channel for isolated black holes.

## disrupt · Disrupted binaries

The binary comes unbound at the first or second collapse when the kick and/or mass loss is violent enough to disrupt the system.

## single · Initially single

Born without a companion and never has a binary partner. Often low-mass black holes.

## bhstar-m · BH+star mergers

The black hole survives its formation kicks, then later merges with its companion after unstable [[mass transfer]] or a failed [[common envelope]].

## dco-m · DCO mergers

Rare double-[[compact object]] mergers. This can be two black holes merging, two neutron stars merging, or one of each.

## bhbh · Detached BH-BH

This is the most common binary configuration for a black hole to be found in. These consist of two black holes orbiting each other with a large seperation between them.

## otherbin · BH + other

Meaning BH+[[WD|white dwarf]], BH+NS, and BH+star. These (while quite rare) are the current main way of observing black holes.
